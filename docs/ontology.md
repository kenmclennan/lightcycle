# Ontology

The single source of truth for lightcycle's vocabulary. Every term used in the code, the specs, the step markdown, and in conversation should match a term here. Coining a synonym for an existing term is drift - fix the usage, or change this doc deliberately.

## The model (nouns)

- **item** and **step** are the two structures work is made of, in their own tables, with no shared shape. "Node" survives only as a loose word for "an item or a step" in a few command names; nothing in the model is one.
- **item** - a unit of deliverable work, and the top of the tree. Carries the `description` (the brief), the artifacts, the `repo`, the `project`, and the workflow pin. Has no parent. Once closed, carries an optional `note` explaining why - write-once via `lc done --note` or `lc close --note`, distinct from a step's own accumulated `notes`.
- **project** - a free-text grouping label on an item (`Node.project`), independent of `repo`: one project may span several repos. Set via `lc new --project` (which also resolves the value against the project registry below, to pick an id shortcode) or `lc set --project` (which resolves and refuses the same way). `lc backlog --project` and `lc retro --project` resolve against the registry before filtering too, refusing an unregistered or ambiguous ref and accepting either the registered short name or the full `owner/name` identity. Every "PROJECT" column and `--project` filter reads this field. Distinct from the registry's own use of the word "project" (below) and from the workflow `workspace: project` keyword (see "The workflow"), which means "this stage's own `repo`" and is unrelated to this field.
- **step** - a single action performed at one workflow **stage**, filed from the workflow. Its `item` is required and fixed at creation. Carries the `role`, the claim, the `notes`, its `reflection`, and its `park`. It has no description and no workflow of its own. It records the step that routed to it (`previous_step`), its **report**, the path of its **log** (`log_path`), and the **frozen inputs** it was handed; the artifacts it attaches are **outputs**, **records** or **bookkeeping**.
- **wiki-link** - the reference form a description, or goal log body may carry: `[[ID]]`, where ID is an item, step or goal id. The TUI resolves it to `Title (ID)` (or `ID (not found)` when nothing resolves) and renders `##` headings as headings; the CLI (`lc show`, `lc goal show`) prints the source text raw, because that text is what the driver edits and agents parse. Anything else in double brackets stays literal.
- **planned step** - a not-yet-filed future step, derived by walking an item's pinned workflow graph forward from its current step along the normal-completion edge. Display-only: never a real node, never claimed or advanced. Represented in code as `ProjectedStep`.
- **artifact** - a workflow-defined value attached to an item, or to a step (see **output**, **record** and **bookkeeping**): `spec`, `design`, `findings`, and whatever a personal origin invents. The engine reads none of them by name. What it does know is a field: the brief is the item's `description`, the target repo its `repo`, an agent's feedback the step's `reflection`, and the branch, PR and comment ledger belong to the phase run.
- **output** - an artifact a step attaches whose type its stage declares under `produces`, and which was not attached `--internal`. Declared outputs are promoted to the item at read time (no row moves): for one identity (type and label) the newest version is current and the earlier versions are history, each carrying its producing step and pass. A reader in pass n sees outputs from its own pass or earlier; an artifact attached to the item itself always wins over a step's.
- **report** - the `--note` a step closed with, stored on the step that wrote it (`steps.report`) and nothing else. It is shown as the step's own `outputs.report` and as `inputs.previous.report` of the step that follows. The old forwarded `from <stage> (<outcome>): ...` text survives only as a compatibility shim on the receiving step's surfaced `notes`.
- **record** - a row a step attached that is neither a declared output nor bookkeeping: a reflection, guidance citations, an undeclared attach, or an `--internal` attach of a declared type. Shown under `records` on the step.
- **bookkeeping** - engine-owned rows on a step (a fixed type list: `session-id`, `trusted-authors`, `pr-head-sha`, `ci-judged-sha`, `admitted-comments`, the behind marker, `schedule-state`, `resolves`, `resolved-by`, and the legacy `log`). They appear in none of `inputs`, `outputs`, `records` or `detail`; only `lc trace` shows them.
- **frozen inputs** - the input set the engine delivered to a step at claim (or at script start, or at creation for a human step): the item's context, the item's outputs so far (by value) and the previous step's outputs and report. Stored once in `step_inputs` and returned verbatim by `lc show <step>` however the item changes afterwards. A step with no stored inputs (never claimed, or closed before inputs were frozen) returns them reconstructed from the item's current state with `frozen: false`, and the TUI labels them reconstructed.
- **role** - who performs a step: `agent`, `human`, or `engine`, and nothing else. It decides only whether the pool may claim the step - an `engine`-owned step is never claimed by a worker; the engine completes it directly on its own per-tick poll. **Which** work a step is is its `stage`, resolved through the workflow graph when it is needed - never copied onto the step.
- **outcome** - how a step ended, and what drives the next transition: `done`, `approved`, `changes`, `rejected`, `drafted`, `merged`, `abandoned`, `conflicted`, `resolved`, `escalate`, `ci-failed`, `gave-up`, `findings`, `clean`, `reviewed`, `approve`, `reject`, `head-moved`. `approve` and `reject` are the hold step's outcomes; `head-moved` is the name the built-in bundles give the `pr_head_moved` hook outcome, which the engine uses to close a merge gate whose PR head has moved. Which outcomes a step accepts is declared by its workflow.
- **disposition** - an engine-owned classification of how a closed **item** ended: `completed` or `abandoned`. Rides beside `outcome` rather than splitting it - `outcome` is the workflow's own routing word, `disposition` answers only "did this item's run of work succeed." A workflow bundle declares the mapping per outcome name (its `disposition:` section); `lc done <item> <outcome>` refuses to close, asking for `--disposition` explicitly, when the outcome isn't bundle-declared. Steps carry no disposition - `outcome` alone already answers everything a step needs to.
- **closed** - an item that has ended, whatever the ending: the superset of **completed**. Already the established meaning elsewhere in this doc ("Once closed, carries an optional `note`", `lc close`, `lc reopen`, `closed_at`).
- **completed** - a closed item that reached its intended end, as opposed to one abandoned along the way: the subset of **closed**. On a clean day the two counts are equal; an abandoned item shows up as `closed` exceeding `completed`, which is how an abandonment stays visible without a separate label naming it.
- **state** - a node's single lifecycle position, one label per what the node is waiting on: `backlogged` (nothing - not activated), `blocked` (another item or step's unresolved dependency), `queued` (a worker slot), `running` (a worker, right now), `waiting` (the human), `done` (nothing - terminal). One state machine (there is no separate `status`). See [state-lifecycle.md](state-lifecycle.md) for the precedence an item's steps resolve through when they disagree.
- **escalation** - a step that reached `waiting` because a park handed it to a human, as opposed to a routine gate a workflow routes to a human owner by design (e.g. `spec-await-merge`, `code-await-merge`). Both read as `waiting`, but only a park is an escalation: something an agent expected to finish on its own instead needed a human decision. The only call in the engine that can produce a `waiting` `history` row is `park_step.py`'s `reassign(step, "human")`; a step created with a human owner is inserted directly into `waiting` at creation without going through that call, so it never writes a history row at all. Counting `history` rows with `state='waiting'` therefore counts exactly the escalations, not routine gate arrivals - the distinction is a fact about which write path produced the row, not a separate field on the step. The TUI and `lc inbox` classify separately: a human-owned step whose step file declares `escalation: true` in its frontmatter (read alongside `engine`/`model`, carried on `StepDef.escalation`) is shown as an escalation, not a routine gate, because a workflow only routes there when something went wrong; it gains no `unblock` outcome, since it routes onward through its own edges.
- **slow step** - a done step whose `active_seconds` is at least `max(2 x p90, 600s)` of the other done steps at the same stage (matched by stage name only, `active_seconds > 0`, the step itself excluded so it cannot raise its own bar), once that stage has at least 20 such other steps; a stage with less history is never flagged. A pure read over steps already in the store (`domain/work/slow_step.py`), shown as the `Slow Steps` row and one row per step on the Report tab for the day the step closed - a passive surface, not an inbox item, and not a park. Each carries a kind: `waiting` when its turns per active minute are at most half the stage's median rate, `working` when above, and `unknown` when it has no recorded turns or the stage's history has none, so missing data never reads as `working`. `active_seconds` accrues only while a live worker holds the step, so time waiting on a human gate is not counted.
- **upgrade finding** - a `lc doctor` finding that reports operator-owned state an engine upgrade left behind, carrying a stable `kind` and a `fix`; reported only, never fixed by the engine.
- **park** - what a step carries when an agent hands it to a human: the `reason` it stopped, what the human `needs` to decide, and what it already `tried`. Parking is what makes an escalation legible at a glance. Resuming the step clears all three - the condition is spent - but first folds them into a `PARK RESOLVED:` line on the step's `notes`, so what the agent found outlives the pause it found it in.
- **lane** - a derived view over `state` alone: `inbox` (`waiting`), `active` (`running`), `queue` (`backlogged`/`blocked`/`queued`), `done`. Lanes are computed, never stored.

## The lifecycle (verbs)

- **activate** - move an item from `backlogged` into the flow by filing its entry step. Realized as `lc set <item> --state active`.
- **return to backlog** - put an activated item back in `backlogged` by removing its open steps. Realized as `lc set <item> --state backlogged`; the open pass and open run are left as they are, so activating again continues them. Refused while a worker holds one of its steps or an open run has a branch or PR (use `lc close` to drop the item), because a PR left on a backlogged item's run can still be resolved by the merge resolver, which would close the item.
- **new** - create a node (`lc new item|step`). `lc new step "<title>"` keeps the positional title argument for symmetry with `lc new item`, but refuses a non-blank one before creating anything - a step has nowhere to store one.
- **set** - update an item's or a step's fields. Each structure accepts only its own: an item takes `--title`, `--description`, `--project`, `--workflow`, `--backlog`, `--step`, `--depends` and `--state active|backlogged`; a step takes `--notes`, `--needs`, `--reason`, `--tried` and `--state ready|waiting`. Anything else is refused at the boundary, before the store is touched, naming what owns the field. A step's item is never among them, and neither is a step's title - a step has no stored title of its own; it is composed at render time from its stage and its item's title, and `--title` on a step is refused the same way a field belonging to the other structure is. An empty string is never a value: setting `description`, `project`, `workflow`, `notes`, `title` or `label` to `""` is refused outright; clearing a field that can legitimately be blank uses `--unset <field>` instead (repeatable), covering `description`, `project` and `workflow` on an item and `notes` on a step. `--depends` (repeatable) is accepted only alongside `--state active`: it records the freshly-filed entry step's dependencies in the same commit as the step itself, so the step is never observable as `queued`/`waiting` before its blockers are in place.
- **attach** - add an artifact to an item (`lc attach`); `--replace` swaps a same-type artifact.
- **dep** - declare one node blocks another (`lc dep <id> --needs <id>`). A dependency is a gate checked once, at the instant a step is claimed - not a continuously-enforced invariant. `--needs` refuses, naming the current claim, when the target is a step that is currently claimed (`claimed_by` set and not yet `done`); there is no way to stop or release that running step other than the pool operator killing the worker. `--remove` is unaffected by a claim either way.
- **claim** - a worker atomically takes the next queued step for a role (`lc claim agent`). With one agent role, any worker takes any queued agent step, whatever its stage. One claim per spawnid, enforced: a further claim from the same spawnid resumes its still-open step if that step is still `running` and still `claimed_by` it, or is refused once that step has closed, been reassigned, or gone - never a fresh, unrelated step.
- **done** - close a node with an outcome (`lc done <id> <outcome>`); a step's outcome advances the flow.
- **close** - a driver-facing verb to end an **item** outright (`lc close <item> [--outcome] [--disposition] [--note]`), a thinner front over the same close-item path `lc done` uses for an item. `--outcome` is free-form text, not validated against a fixed set (default `closed`); `--disposition` defaults to `completed`. Refuses a **step**, naming `lc done <step> <outcome>` as the verb for one - a step's outcome drives routing, so it is never "closed" outright.
- **reopen** - undo a closed **item** outright (`lc reopen <item>`), clearing its outcome and close time so its state rolls up fresh from its children again - landing back in `backlogged` immediately, whether the item never had children or its old ones are all done, since neither leaves anything live underneath it. It does not, by itself, put the item back to work: resuming it is filing a step directly against it, or reactivating it (`lc set <item> --state active`), a separate action either way - both work the same regardless of whether the item had children before. Refuses a **step** (`--state ready` hands one back to its lane instead), a non-item node, or an item that isn't closed.
- **advance** - file the next step for an outcome without closing (plumbing).
- **github poll** - the background read of GitHub the pool runs every `github-poll-seconds` (`GithubPollUseCase`, started by the tick after the PR monitor on a `GithubPollRunnerPort` daemon thread). Per repo that has a watched PR it lists recently updated PRs once, then fully reads only the PRs whose `updated_at` moved, that a new inline comment named, whose listed state differs from the snapshot's, or whose base branch tip moved. It also re-reads every open PR whose last view was `UNKNOWN` (GitHub still computing mergeability) on every run until it settles, and refreshes each watched PR's view once every `github-view-refresh-polls` runs (the **view refresh**, spread by a hash of the PR url) as a safety net for changes no trigger sees. A read that fails is written to `run.log` as a `github` line, one per failed read per poll. Each run first performs the queued **pr writes**, then reads. Named "poll" because **sweep** already means orphan reclaim.
- **gh call log** - one line per `gh` invocation, appended by a `gh` shim (`adapters/gh_shim.py`, written to `<data home>/shims/gh`) that the spawner puts first on a worker's `PATH` and `lc start` puts first on the pool's own. A worker's lines go to `logs/gh-calls-<spawnid>.log` (source = the spawn id); any process with no `LC_SPAWNID` writes `logs/gh-calls-pool-<YYYYMMDD>.log` (source `pool`). A line is `<epoch>\t<source>\t<exit code>\t<first four argv words>`; the shim runs the real `gh` with the same arguments and stdin and passes its exit code through. Each tick `GhCallAccrualUseCase` counts a bound worker's lines and writes the absolute count per spawn to the `step_gh_calls` table, which sums into `steps.usage_api_calls` (the "API calls" figure beside tokens and cost; `NULL` for a step never metered). A line is one invocation, not one budget unit, so the count is a floor. A worker's file is removed when its worker record is pruned, and the pool's daily files keep only the newest `worker-history` after each sweep; `lc rm` clears a removed step's `step_gh_calls` and `step_tool_usage` rows.
- **github budget** - the core `limit`, `remaining` and `reset` (epoch seconds) of the GitHub API budget, read by the **github poll** with one free `gh api rate_limit` call per cycle and published through `GithubBudgetStatusPort` to a status file in the data home's logs directory (so the TUI, `lc doctor` and a worker's `lc claim` can see what only the pool process reads). A failed read leaves the file unchanged. Shown in the TUI status bar and by `lc doctor`.
- **github reserve** - the `github-reserve-calls` floor. The budget is *low* while `now < reset` and `remaining < github-reserve-calls`; no reading yet is not low. While low the tick spawns no worker for a **github-heavy** step, `lc claim` skips one, and the run log says `github   budget low, N remaining, resets at T` once on opening and `budget recovered` once on clearing. The poll keeps running so a fresh reading releases the hold.
- **github-heavy** - a step front-matter key `github: heavy` marking a stage whose runs make many `gh` calls (the only accepted value; `lc workflow check --dir` rejects any other and a bundle using it without `requires-engine`). Read into `StepDef.github_heavy`; not hardcoded in the engine.
- **always-run** - a step front-matter key `always-run: true` marking an agent stage the engine must never close on the agent's behalf (the only accepted value; `lc workflow check --dir` rejects any other and a bundle using it without `requires-engine`). Read into `StepDef.always_run`, kept out of the claim `config`. It exempts the stage from the skip described under **watch-ci**.
- **pr writes** - the in-memory queue (`PrWrites`) the tick fills and the **github poll** drains. The tick requests a **branch update** for a `(pr, head)` (a second identical request is coalesced); the poll's next run checks the base's up-to-date rule (cached 300 s from when the check was performed) and calls update-branch, then publishes an outcome - updated, update-failed, not-required or rule-failed - that the tick takes and applies on a later tick. The tick never waits on either call. It also records, per PR, the head SHAs it updated from (`pool_updated_from`), so the poll can tell a pool update from a push it did not cause. The **github poll** use case seeds these records from the store and drains new ones back to it on the tick thread.
- **feedback cutoff** - the time before which a PR's comments, inline threads and reviews are not read as feedback (`created_at > cutoff`, strictly). It is the push time of the last head the pool did not cause: a head change the pool made through a **branch update** (recorded in **pr writes**) carries the cutoff forward instead of advancing it to the rebase time, so feedback posted before the rebase is not dropped; any other push (a human rebase, an agent's rework push) moves it. The **pr snapshot** holds it per PR, and every feedback step the dispatch opens carries it as an internal `feedback-cutoff` artifact. The cutoff and the heads the pool updated from persist in the store (`pr_feedback_cutoffs`, `pr_pool_updates`), so a pool restart seeds the carry rule instead of re-baselining it.
- **pr snapshot** - the in-memory `GitHubEventsPort` (`PrSnapshot`) the PR readers read instead of GitHub. The **github poll** fills it; a read it cannot yet answer returns `NotYetRead`, a `ReadFailure` the readers skip without recording a condition, and registers the PR so the next poll reads it. A PR is watched while any reader still asks about it. Its `push_time` is the **feedback cutoff**, and `cutoff_head` is the head SHA the cutoff was computed for. The **github poll** use case seeds it from the store on the tick thread and drains cutoff changes back to it.
- **sweep** - reclaim orphaned or stalled step claims and prune dead worker records.
- **reclaim** - return a stalled or dead worker's step to `queued`.
- **stalled** - a claimed worker, past its boot window, whose log has not grown for longer than `stall-seconds` and has not yet issued a terminal command; killed on the next sweep.
- **holding** - a **pool**-level state, not an item or step state: the pool is taking on no new work because of memory. True when the memory gate's last recorded admission cap is zero (it will admit no further worker). Shown as `⏸ holding` in the TUI footer alongside the alive and `max-agents` counts; every hold is currently attributed to memory. A hold is a zero admission cap; holding never stops the pool outright, because the gate admits one worker whenever none is working.

- **draining** - a **pool**-level state: `lc stop --drain` (or the TUI drain option) has set the drain marker, so the pool admits no new worker and exits once no worker is alive and no engine step (such as a CI watch) is in flight. Reaping, the sweep, the PR monitor and the cadence gates keep running, so running work finishes and closes normally. Distinct from **holding**, which is memory-driven and never ends the pool. A plain `lc stop` overrides a drain.
- **probe cooldown** - after the breaker's own rate-limit probe is killed for stalling, `reset_at` is re-armed `probe-cooldown-seconds` forward rather than left at its stale value; a stall is inconclusive, never treated as a successful probe.
- **spin** - a worker that dies before producing any model output (no `assistant` or `result` event in its log), tracked as a consecutive-death streak. A step whose worker dies this way `spin-cap` times in a row is parked for a human instead of reclaimed again; if at least two workers holding different steps die this way together in one check, the pool caps itself to one concurrent worker until real activity is observed, with no cooldown timer.
- **retro** - gather an item's step feedback and signals into a digest.
- **content regression** - a PR's head advancing since the last poll but dropping a file the previously-pinned head touched; caught by comparing each poll's head against a stored `content-pin` and routed to a human rather than merged silently.
- **read** - `show` (one node as JSON), `trace` (an item end-to-end: artifacts + child steps + logs), `status` / `inbox` / `backlog` / `active` / `queue` (lane views), `flow` (the assembled workflow), `worklog`, `search` (text match over title/description/notes across every state, including done), `peek` (a stage's step guidance as currently pinned for a given item/step's workflow origin).

## The workflow (how steps chain)

- **workflow** - the graph, defined in markdown: `entry`, `requires` (artifacts the item must carry to start), `provides` (artifact types assumed available for graph-validity checking, without also demanding they be attached before activation - e.g. `spec`, produced mid-flow by the spec phase rather than attached upfront), `workspace` (which repo a stage runs in), `nodes` (stage -> step file), `edges` (outcome -> next step), `hooks` (external event -> transition), `signals`, `display` (stage -> human-facing phrase).
- **workflow** (second sense, TUI-only) - an item's own executed workflow: the ordered sequence of steps it has actually run - done, in progress, or filed but blocked - shown in the hub's Workflow tab. Distinct from the graph definition above; context disambiguates the two, since the tab is always scoped to one item - "this item's Workflow" means the executed sequence, while "workflow" unqualified elsewhere in this doc means the graph.
- **step file** / **step markdown** - the prompt for a stage (`steps/<name>.md`, in a workflow source). Workflow policy and conventions live here; the engine stays agnostic.
- **prompt fragment** - a prompt component under the engine's `prompts/fragments/` or an origin's own `fragments/` (a pinned bundle's fragment wins over the engine's on a name collision), included into a step by an `@include <name>` line that `read_md` resolves, so a rule such as `plain-language` has one home.
- **plain-language caps** - rules 2 and 3 of the plain-language standard, counted at write time: at most 3 paragraphs, 180 words and 4 sentences per paragraph, and at most 30 words per sentence (`domain/work/plain_language_caps.py`). Checked on `lc done --note`, `lc close --note`, `lc new step --note` and the `--needs`, `--reason` and `--tried` of `lc set --state waiting`, before the command's use case runs; a breach is refused with exit `2` and nothing stored, and the message quotes each offending sentence. Code spans, fenced blocks, double-quoted text and single-quoted multi-word text are excluded from the count. A reflection is excluded because no person reads it cold; the retro audit reads the checked fields through `lc show` instead.
- **entry** - the step filed when an item is activated.
- **edge** - `step  outcome  next-step`; a `next-step`-less edge declares the outcome terminal (closes, no new step).
- **primary edge** - `step  outcome  next-step  primary`; marks which of a stage's outgoing outcomes is its main line, for a consumer that must pick one edge among several with no hook backing to disambiguate them (e.g. the simulate coverage planner's tie-break, or planned-step projection's normal-completion edge). It is workflow-authored content like every other `edges:` token - the engine does not know or care what the marked outcome is named, and it never changes a real step's routing.
- **hook** - an external event (a PR merge, close, or comment) mapped to a transition.
- **gate** - a human step that must close before downstream proceeds (e.g. the spec-phase `await-merge`, the spec-PR review gate).
- **signal** - a per-step counter or condition feeding cadence or escalation.
- **phase** - a group of stages sharing one PR and one worktree (spec, feature, code). It names _which_ gate, not when.
- **workspace** - which repo a stage's worktree is cut from. `project` (the default) is the item's own `repo` artifact; `specs` and any other value name a registered project, resolved through the project registry. An unregistered name is an error, never a silent fall back to the item's repo.
- **pass** - one traversal of the workflow for an item. A workflow that loops runs several; a stage's `pass-end:` declares where a traversal ends. The boundary is declared rather than derived because a rework back-edge and a delivery back-edge are the same shape in the graph. Ids read `<item>.p1`, `<item>.p2`.
- **phase run** - one phase within one pass, keyed on `(item, pass, phase)` and ided `<item>.p1.spec`. A run owns one branch, one PR, and that PR's comment ledger - `comments_dispatched_through` (the engine, at spawn) beside `comments_handled_through` (the agent, at completion). While a run is open, further activity in its phase reuses its branch and worktree rather than minting another; a later pass through the same phase is a different run, which is why nothing can resolve back to an earlier pass's already-merged PR. Phase alone identifies a gate, never a pass.
- **display phrase** - an optional, per-stage human-facing label declared in a workflow's `display:` block. Shown alongside or in place of the raw stage name wherever a human scans (`lc inbox`, `lc status`, the TUI); never a substitute for the stage name itself, which stays the identifier `lc claim` and every JSON payload use. A stage with no declared phrase falls back to its bare name.
- **Outputs tab** - the item hub's second tab (Description, Outputs, Workflow, Cost): one row per output name from `ItemOutputs.for_item`, plus one `pr` row per phase from the item's phase runs, each showing the current value, the producing step and the pass. A row with earlier versions shows `+N` and expands under itself (space), dim and indented; any version opens in the artifact viewer. Records and bookkeeping never appear. The human step view shows the same rows for its item. Built by `domain/work/output_rows.py`; it replaced the Artifacts tab.
- **item context block** - the key-value lines the item hub's Description tab shows above the description text: `repo`, `workflow`, `goal` (one per goal linking the item), `resolves` (one per backlog item it resolves, `ID (not found)` when the id no longer resolves) and, once the item is done, its closing `note`. A field with no value is omitted, and with none the tab is the description alone. Read by `ItemContextReader` (`application/work/item_context.py`); goals have no reverse lookup, so it scans `list_goals` and `goal_items`.
- **detail** - an optional step front-matter key listing the rows a step view's Detail tab shows (`outcome`, `pr`, `in:<name>`, `out:<name>`, and so on); without it a default list applies. The Detail tab of a step in a run always leads with the run rows - `pr`, `spec-pr` (the pass's spec-phase PR, on a step outside that run), `branch`, `worktree`, `spec` (the item's spec artifact, when present) and `ci-checks` (the PR checks URL for the step's or its previous step's `ci-judged-sha`) - and a step's `detail:` list adds to them, a name in both shown once, leading. A parked step's Detail always leads with `reason` and `needs` whatever the step declares, because a park is engine-owned state a bundle cannot hide.
- **presents** - an optional step front-matter key on a human gate naming what the human acts on. When the step declares `detail`, it names one of its detail entries (checked by `presents_problems` in `flow_contracts.py`); without `detail`, the legacy forms apply: `pr`, `description`, or `artifact:<name>`. It names the subject, never an interface tab, so workflows stay interface-agnostic. Validated with the bundle: any other value, `presents` on a non-human step, or an `artifact:` no step in the workflow `produces` is an authoring error. `presents: report` on a human gate is also refused: a gate's own `report` is its closing note, null while it is open, so present `in:report` for the previous step's report.
- **focus** - what a step view opens on, resolved once in the application layer from the step's kind and state, its `presents`, its resolved `detail` entries and its outputs: `log`, `reason`, `detail`, `context` or `workflow` (`description` for an item), each carrying the name of the row to select. Interface-neutral; the TUI maps a focus to a tab and `lc inbox` or the driver can read the same answer. A legacy `presents` (`pr`, `description`, `artifact:<name>`) lands on the matching Detail entry (`pr`, `in:description`, `in:<name>`); a landing falls to Context and then Workflow when Detail has no value, so it never targets an empty tab.
- **openable detail row** - a detail row whose value is a single `http://` or `https://` URL (no space, one line, decided by `detail_value_is_url` in `domain/work/detail.py`) opens in the browser from the TUI Detail tab, whatever its key. `pr` always opens in the browser and `worktree` in the editor.

## The spec-driven pipeline (steps)

Every stage name is an **action**. The stage and its markdown file are the same word by default; the `nodes:` block maps a stage to a differently named file. The role is not one of these names - it is `agent`, `human`, or `engine`. The built-in `spec-driven` workflow is one arc: a brief becomes a formal spec on a spec PR, and once that PR merges the same item continues into the code build. `open-pr` and `await-merge` each appear twice (the spec phase and the code phase).

- **spec-writer** (agent) - author the formal spec from the item's description, on a branch in the specs repo.
- **open-pr** (agent) - push the branch and open the PR (used at both the spec and code phases).
- **await-merge** (human) - you review and merge the PR; lightcycle never merges for you. The spec-phase `await-merge` is the review gate; merging it advances the SAME item into the code phase (no workflow flip).
- **write-code** (agent) - implement the merged spec on a branch in the project repo.
- **poll-ci** (engine) - the engine judges the PR snapshot's head SHA and the check-runs the GitHub poll read for it, every tick, until CI concludes; no worker is ever spawned for it. On success the stage it routes to may be closed by the engine without a worker (see **watch-ci**). Before routing `failed` it re-reads the snapshot's head and, if the head moved or cannot be read, leaves the step queued to read again next tick. It also holds the judgement until a poll that began after it first saw the step has confirmed the PR's view, so a step created just after a push never judges the previous head's cached checks. The judge decides on the checks the base branch requires when it declares any, and on every check otherwise (also when none of the PR's checks match the declared set); a judged check that stays `in_progress` past the check ceiling routes `failed` with a note naming it.
- **required checks** - the context names the base branch's rules and branch protection require, read by the GitHub poll, cached per base for `RULE_TTL_SECONDS` and published to the snapshot; `poll-ci` treats them as the judged set and every other check as informational.
- **check ceiling** - `ci-check-ceiling-seconds`: how long a judged check may stay `in_progress` before `poll-ci` calls it timed out and routes `failed`.
- **watch-ci** (agent) - investigate a CI conclusion the engine already read: comment handling always, plus log-fetching and judgement on failure. The engine closes it `done` itself, with a note on the step saying it was skipped, when `poll-ci` judged success, the head has not moved, the feedback reads all succeeded and found no comment, inline thread or review from a trusted author newer than the feedback cutoff, and no approved held comment exists; the item then lands on the stage's `done` target as if an agent had closed it. The rule keys on structure (the agent stage reached by a `ci_success` outcome that has a `done` route), not on the name `watch-ci`. A failure, any feedback, an unconfirmed or failed read, a claimed step, or `always-run: true` on the stage leaves it for an agent.
- **review-code** (agent) - review the code on the PR.
- **handle-feedback** (agent) - interpret PR feedback (`@lc` mention or a review bot) and decide rework / answer / ignore.
- **cleanup** (agent) - remove the worktree and branch; terminal.
- **resolve-conflict** (agent) - rebase and resolve merge conflicts.
- **review-conflict** (human) - the escalation endpoint when conflicts cannot be resolved.
- **review-ci** (human) - the escalation endpoint when CI keeps failing past the cap instead of looping write-code forever.

The periodic retro **audit** and the **daily summary** are scheduled workflows (`lightcycle/retro-audit`, `lightcycle/daily-summary`), not engine services: a `schedule-<id>` config block files each behind a guard, and a fresh config seeds the blocks. The items they file are labelled `schedule-origin`, `schedule:<id>` and `schedule-guard:<guard>`, which is how the Automation counts count them as audits and daily summaries. Completing a scheduled item whose `schedule-state` is `batch: <ids>` marks that batch `retroed`; one whose state is `day: <date> count: <n>` writes its `summary` artifact as that day's summary for the Report tab. The engine keeps its old `audit`, `material-audit`, `review-findings` and `daily-summary` steps only so an item already open on one can finish; nothing files a new one.

## Deployment (engine, data home, workflow sources)

- **engine** - the installed package (pipx venv): the code plus `prompts/` (the engine-owned agent prompts it spawns directly - `prompts/steps/audit.md`, the retro auditor, `prompts/steps/material-audit.md`, the material auditor, and `prompts/steps/daily-summary.md`, the daily summariser). Replaced wholesale by `lc upgrade`. The engine ships no workflow library; workflows are pulled.
- **data home** (`~/.lightcycle`, named by `LC_HOME`) - the store (`store.db`), config, logs, worktrees, and the pulled workflow bundles under `workflows/<origin>/<sha>/`. Never touched by `lc upgrade`.
- **engine requirement** - the optional `requires-engine` key in a workflow source's `source.toml`: one or more comma-separated clauses (`>=`, `>`, `<=`, `<` plus a three-integer version, for example `>=0.6.318,<0.7.0`) naming the engine releases the bundle works with. `lc workflow add`/`upgrade` refuse a bundle the running engine does not satisfy, `lc upgrade` refuses to install an engine an installed bundle excludes (`--force` overrides), and `lc doctor` reports it. Absent means no requirement. It is finer than `contract`, which is a grammar generation, not a release.
- **remaining stages** - for an in-flight item, the stages its workflow can still reach: every stage reachable by edge or hook target (`pr_feedback`, `ci_failed_cap`, `review_rounds_cap`) from the stages of its open steps, or from the graph `entry` when none is open. A queued, waiting or blocked step's own stage counts; a running step's does not, since it read its text at claim, unless a rework or loop edge returns to it. Computed by `remaining_stages` over both the pinned and the current graph, and decides whether a changed step is "ahead" for the repin and for `lc doctor`'s `pins` check.
- **workflow source** - a git repo (an **origin**) holding a `source.toml` manifest plus `workflows/*.md` and `steps/*.md`. The engine pulls it into an immutable, sha-pinned **bundle**; each item pins `<origin>/<name>@<sha>` at activation, and the loader resolves the flow and steps from that pin. Managed with `lc workflow add|upgrade|set|list|rm`. `lc workflow upgrade` repins each in-flight item of the moved origin that has a changed step among its **remaining stages** (`--no-repin` skips this). There is no `.lightcycle/` step/workflow override and no resolution chain - a pinned bundle is self-contained.
- **lifecycle hook** - a script a workflow declares in a `lifecycle:` front-matter block (`install`, `upgrade`, `maintain`, plus `cron` for `maintain`), each a path under the bundle's `scripts/`. `install` runs when `lc workflow add` first brings in a workflow that declares it (or when `lc workflow upgrade` first sees a workflow declare it), `upgrade` when `lc workflow upgrade` moves the origin's SHA, `maintain` from the pool tick on its cron. A hook is run directly with the workflow data directory as its working directory and a timeout (`lifecycle-timeout-seconds`); it receives `LC_WORKFLOW_*` environment variables (hook, origin, name, data directory, bundle directory, SHA, `LC_WORKFLOW_FROM_SHA`/`LC_WORKFLOW_TO_SHA` for `upgrade`, and one `LC_WORKFLOW_CONFIG_<KEY>` per declared config key that is set). A failing `install` or `upgrade` leaves the origin as it was; hooks must be idempotent. Installing an origin means trusting its code, as with a Claude Code plugin.
- **script stage** - a stage whose step file names `script: scripts/<file>` in its front matter. The engine owns the stage (owner `engine`, no agent, no tokens): the pool tick runs the bundle script in the workflow data directory (or the item's worktree, which the engine creates when the stage declares a workspace), and completes the step with the outcome the script prints. The step stays `QUEUED` while the script runs, so `lc stop --drain` waits for it. Delivery is at-least-once, so scripts must be idempotent.
- **script guard** - a `guard: script:scripts/<file>` line in a `schedule:` block in place of an engine guard. The pool starts it in the background when the schedule is due and, when it finishes, files an item from its `pass` result (title, description, `state`) or files nothing on `fail` or a contract violation.
- **script contract** - what a script stage or script guard receives and prints. Environment: the lifecycle hook variables (without `LC_WORKFLOW_HOOK`) plus `LC_WORKFLOW_SCRIPT` (`stage` or `guard`), `LC_ITEM`/`LC_STEP`/`LC_STAGE` for a stage, `LC_SCHEDULE_ID` and `LC_SCHEDULE_PARAM_<KEY>` for a guard. Stdin carries one JSON object (the item's and step's non-internal artifacts plus the item's `schedule_state` - its guard's recorded `schedule-state`, or `null` for a hand-filed item - for a stage, the schedule and its last `schedule-state` for a guard). Stdout is one JSON object: `{"outcome", "artifacts", "note"}` for a stage, `{"outcome": "pass"|"fail", "title", "description", "state", "note"}` for a guard. A non-zero exit, a timeout (`script-timeout-seconds`), unparseable output, an undeclared or missing outcome or an unknown key is a violation: a stage parks with the stderr tail, a guard warns and files nothing.
- **run log** - the file a script stage, script guard or lifecycle hook run writes at `logs/<script-|guard-|hook->...-<epoch ms>.log` in the data home. It holds a header (script path, start time), a `stderr` section streamed while the script runs, a `stdout` section appended at exit, and a footer with the exit code and duration (or the timeout / could-not-start line). A script stage records its path in the step's `log_path` field (the legacy `log` bookkeeping row is not how the log is recorded), as do the other two kinds below, so `lc logs`, `lc trace` and the TUI Log tab show it, live while the step is `QUEUED` and as a file after; a script guard and a lifecycle hook run record it the same way, in the `log_path` of the step they file or fail on (the maintenance failure item's step, for a hook), and also name it in their warning or failure message and in `lc doctor`. After each run, only the newest `worker-history` run logs of that kind are kept; worker logs (`worker-*.log`) are not pruned by anything.
- **workflow data directory** - `<data_root>/workflow-data/<origin>/<workflow>/`, created before a workflow's first hook runs and kept across upgrades and `lc workflow rm`. The engine never decides what lives in it. `lc workflow purge <origin> [<workflow>]` removes it.
- **workflow config block** - the `workflow-<origin>-<name>:` block in the main config, shaped like a `schedule-<id>` block and reloaded live. The workflow declares the keys it reads in a `config:` block (each `required` or `optional`) and supplies `config-example:` values that `lc workflow check` and `lc workflow simulate` use in place of the live block. `lc doctor` flags a missing or blank required key.
- **maintenance failure item** - the item the pool tick files when a `maintain` run exits non-zero or times out: one engine step `review-lifecycle-failure` in `lc inbox`, labelled `lifecycle-origin` and `lifecycle:<origin>/<name>`. While one is open for a workflow, further failures file nothing; `lc done <step> done` closes it. A successful run files nothing.
- **writable origin** - a workflow origin the user can edit, recorded as a `writable` flag in its registration. `lc workflow add --writable` and `lc workflow init` set it; `lc workflow set <origin> --writable|--read-only` changes it; `lc workflow upgrade` never does. A registration with no `writable` key reads as read-only, the fail-safe direction. Nothing reads the flag yet; a later routing step will use it to send a finding to a destination this machine can act on.
- **developer mode** - the `developer-mode` config key (`LC_DEVELOPER_MODE`, seeded `false`): whether this machine develops lightcycle itself. When true, the engine and the plugin count as writable for routing; when false, a finding against them goes upstream. Nothing reads it yet beyond `lc config`.
- **goal** - a record of work bigger than one item and open-ended in a way an item cannot be: a title, a **project** (the registry short name, the same grouping an item carries), a `description` (long prose written by hand), a hand-set **goal status**, an append-only **goal log**, and links to items. A new goal's description is seeded from the engine's goal template (`lightcycle/prompts/templates/goal.md`) when `lc goal new` gets no `--description`; its five sections are Objective, The work in areas, **Open now**, Recently completed and What to pick up next, and the headings are a starting point the driver may change. **Open now** is a short checklist of open work that is not an item (a wait on a named person, an undecided question, a loose end) in plain words, with any id in parentheses only; items stay on the Items tab and are never listed as a checklist, sequence or count. Recently completed is a few plain sentences, never an id list. The engine stores and renders the description and parses none of it. It is not a node: no `type`, no `state`, no history, no parent, and it never appears in `all_nodes()`, the lanes, `claim`, `dep` or roll-up. The flow engine never reads one. Ids are `G-<n>`. Maintained by hand with `lc goal`, a driver surface workers may not run. A worker cannot read a goal, so a brief that depends on a goal's decision copies that decision into its own text and never tells the worker to read the goal.
- **goal status** - a goal's hand-set standing: `not started`, `in progress`, `done` or `archived`, changed only by `lc goal set --status`. Deliberately not `state`: `state` is what a node is waiting on, and a goal waits on nothing. `archived` marks a goal that no longer matters without deleting it: `lc goal list` and the TUI's Goals tab omit an archived goal by default (`lc goal list --all` includes it), while `lc goal show` and goal links still work on one.
- **goal log** - a goal's append-only record of decisions as they are made, newest first. Each entry carries a terse single-line title (what a reader scans down the page) and a body (all the substance). Never a summary, and it has no edit or delete path.
- **state of play** - a section of a goal's `description` that the driver writes and rewrites by hand under a heading of their choosing (the goal template's "What to pick up next" and "Recently completed" sections are where to start, and every heading remains the driver's to change), answering "where are we and what is next". Read wherever the description is - the Overview and `lc goal show` - and never generated: the driver holds the context of the work, so no agent reconstructs it. Written with `lc goal set <goal> --description`.
- **open questions** - not a term of their own: an optional section of a goal's `description`, written by hand like the rest. Resolving one is two acts - edit the description to answer or drop it, and write the `goal log` entry saying what was decided and why.
- **goal link** - a many-to-many association between a goal and an item: an item may serve several goals. Linking is a judgement against the goal's description and reads nothing about the item's state; deleting the item removes its links and leaves the goal and its log.

## Project registry

- **project** - a registered codebase lightcycle files work against: an **identity**, a **shortcode**, a local path, and a remote URL. Stored in the project registry, managed with `lc project add|set|list|rm` (`set` takes `--facets` and `--shortcode`). The shortcode `G` is reserved for goal ids (`G-<n>`): `lc project add --shortcode G` refuses it, and a default that would be `G` falls back to owner plus name uppercased.

  Confusingly shares its name with an item's own `project` field (see "The model (nouns)"): a registered project here is always one repo; an item's `project` is a human-chosen label that may span several. `lc new --project` resolves the value against this registry to pick a shortcode, then stores it verbatim as the item's `project` regardless. When `--project` is omitted, `lc new --repo` resolves against this registry - deriving the item's `project` as the registry's short name for the match - and `lc attach <id> repo <value>` (only while the item's `project` is still unset) does the same, leaving `project` unset if the repo doesn't resolve. At creation the repo must resolve: `lc new item` with an unregistered `--repo` is refused, never minted under a fallback. Beyond these paths, nothing else assumes an item's `project` corresponds to a registered entry.

- **identity** - the canonical `owner/name` string parsed from a repo's GitHub remote (SSH or HTTPS); the registry's lookup key. A path with no parseable GitHub remote has no identity and cannot be registered.
- **facet** - a lowercase kebab-case name a project declares for a language, framework or pattern it uses. An ordered list on the project, set only by `lc project add --facets a,b,c` and `lc project set --facets a,b,c` (which replaces the whole list; an empty value clears it), never inferred. Valid only when the guidance repo has a concept page for it, which `lc doctor` checks.
- **automation item** - an item the engine files for itself rather than a project: a retro **audit**, a **material audit**, a **daily summary**, a **hold step**'s item or an item a **schedule** filed. Membership is decided by label (`retro-origin`, which marks any engine audit item, `summary-origin`, `held-comment-origin`, or `schedule-origin`), never by id prefix; a legacy summary-origin item whose step is `goal-state-of-play` (from the retired `lc goal refresh`) is still an automation item but belongs to no kind and is listed nowhere. Automation items are excluded from Done and from the Report's project figures (`Items Completed`, `Items Closed`, `Spend`), and are listed, with a per-kind tally, on the Automation tab and in the Report's `Automation Items` / `Automation Spend` rows. They stay visible items: nothing suppresses them, only where they are listed differs.
- **schedule** - a config-enabled, optionally bundle-suggested rule that files an item on a workflow and project when a cron cadence is due and its **guard** passes. A workflow's front matter may suggest one (`schedule:` with `cron`, `guard` and the guard's parameters); a suggestion never runs until the operator's config holds a `schedule-<id>` block with `enabled: true`. Config overrides the suggestion field by field. A schedule runs one item at a time (none while an item labelled `schedule:<id>` is open), fires at most once after an outage, and reads cron in local time. The item it files is an ordinary item on the named workflow carrying the labels `schedule-origin` and `schedule:<id>`, so it is an automation item (listed under "Scheduled"), not project work. `enabled: false` records a schedule as off and nothing in the engine turns it back on; the seeded key `schedules-paused` pauses every schedule at once. `lc schedule list` shows each as `on`, `off` or `paused` with the reason (`run requested` while a **run request** is pending); `lc doctor` flags one naming a workflow, guard, cron or project the engine cannot resolve.
- **restart-required key** - one of the four config keys the running pool captures once at start (`poll-seconds`, `pool-upgrade-check-seconds`, `tick-failure-cap`, `spin-cap`; `RESTART_REQUIRED_KEYS` in `config.py`) and so keeps its start value when the file changes. Every other key takes effect when the pool **reloads** the config at a tick boundary after the file's modification time moves; a reload that fails validation keeps the last good config and is reported by `lc doctor`.
- **run request** - a stored mark, set by `lc schedule run <id>`, that makes the pool treat an enabled schedule as due on its next tick. It skips only the cron check; the open-item rule and the guard still apply, and it is cleared once the guard has been evaluated, whether it passes or not.
- **guard** - an engine-defined, read-only check that says whether a schedule has new work, from a fixed set the engine owns (`pending-reflections` with `min`, `materials-changed`, `day-unsummarised`, `repos-active`); never shell or code from a bundle. A failing guard files nothing and spends nothing, and a guard that cannot read its inputs counts as not passed. Each passing guard records a state string on the item it files so it does not refile on the same input.
- **trusted author** - a GitHub login, human or bot, named in the engine config key `trusted-authors` (required, no default, never in a workflow bundle; `lc init` seeds it from the `gh` identity). A comment, review or `@lc` mention from a trusted author may drive redrive-by-comment; the same rule applies on all three routes and compares logins case-insensitively. Bots are trusted by being named in the key; none is trusted by default. Anyone else is held. While the key is unset the pool reads no PR comments.
- **held comment** - a PR comment, review or mention from an author who is not trusted, recorded in the `held_comments` ledger keyed by run and `<kind>:<id>` (`inline`, `review`, `mention`) so it is decided once and never re-offered. States: `held` (awaiting the operator), `approved` (admitted by `approve`; dispatches on a later tick whatever its age), `rejected` (terminal; never re-offered), `dispatched` (terminal; handed to `handle-feedback`). Approval is per comment: a later comment by the same author is held again. A hold on a PR that merges or is abandoned first is closed `reject`.
- **hold step** - the human-owned engine step `review-held-comment` that puts a held comment in `lc inbox`, on its own item labelled `held-comment-origin` and minted under `held-comment-shortcode` (seeded `HLD`). Its outcomes are `approve` and `reject`, and no agent claims it, so the untrusted body is only ever read by the operator. The item is an automation item, listed on the Automation tab under "Held comments".
- **shortcode** - the id prefix items registered under a project nest beneath (see "Identity" below). Explicit at registration (`--shortcode`) or defaulted from identity's name segment, uppercased. Per-project, not engine-wide. There is no global shortcode: `lc new item` requires a project that resolves to one. An unresolvable `--project` (no match, an ambiguous match, or a match with no shortcode) is an error; no `--project` given falls back to `--repo`, when it resolves to a registered project with a shortcode. An unregistered, ambiguous or shortcode-less `--repo`, or neither flag, is refused with the fix named (`lc project add <repo> --shortcode <PREFIX>`, or `--project`), and nothing is created or minted. The store enforces the same rule: `create_item` with neither an explicit id nor a shortcode raises. The engine-wide exceptions are `audit-shortcode` (config), used only for items the engine itself files with no project - never inherited from the items being batched - and `guidance-shortcode` (config, seeded `GDN`), used only to register the guidance project.
- **material audit** - the scheduled workflow (`lightcycle/material-audit`, behind the `materials-changed` guard) that judges one kind of material, today the guidance entries and the installed workflow bundles. It never blocks or is blocked by the retro audit. It files and edits nothing: findings go to a human review stage and the pipeline drafts each change through a `guidance-entry` item. The engine's retained `prompts/steps/material-audit.md` is still what `lc audit-criteria` prints.
- **audit criteria** - the per-material criteria text in the material audit prompt (one `## Material: <token>` section per kind), printed by `lc audit-criteria <material>` so a plugin skill can run the same criteria on demand without keeping a copy.
- **promotion candidate** - a finding or verdict from an audit recommending that a rule move from prose (a step prompt, a guidance entry or a `CLAUDE.md`) to a check. A rule qualifies only when it is mechanically decidable: it names a grep, a count, a file that must exist or a pattern that must not appear, and a tool could enforce it cheaply and without over-matching. The digest records it as a `Promotion candidate:` line naming the repo the check belongs in, the check and the prose it replaces; the check lands first and the same change removes the prose, or for a guidance entry a `guidance-entry` item then sets `status: deprecated`. Both audits ask the question; `review-code` does not.
- **guidance repo** - the registered `guidance` project (with the shortcode from `guidance-shortcode`, so `--project guidance` mints `GDN-` ids): a git repo of guidance entries in the lightcycle OKF profile, created by `lc init` (default `~/workspace/agent-guidance`, override `--guidance-path`) and checked with `lc guidance-dir [--check]`. Its root index.md carries only `okf_version`; CONVENTIONS.md states the profile and records the **guidance conventions** version it was written from (`Conventions version: <N>`).
- **guidance conventions** - the engine-owned rules for a guidance entry: one versioned text (`CONVENTIONS_TEXT` and `CONVENTIONS_VERSION` in `lightcycle/domain/guidance/conventions.py`, written into a new guidance repo's CONVENTIONS.md by `lc init`) and one checker, `lc guidance-check [--dir PATH] [--mode draft|final] [--base REF]`, that enforces its thirteen rules over the `.md` files changed in `BASE...HEAD` (defaults: current directory, `final`, `origin/main`). `lc guidance-conventions` prints the text as `init` writes it. Both are read-only, a worker may run them, and it exits 1 on any failure, on an uncommitted `.md` change in the directory (naming each file; commit first), or when the base cannot be read. When nothing changed against the base it says no guidance files changed and exits 0, never reporting clean. The version is bumped by hand whenever a rule or the text changes; nothing compares an existing repo's recorded version with the engine's.
- **guidance entry** - one file in the guidance repo, `type: Guidance`, naming the facets and steps it `applies-to`. Delivered only when `status: stable` on the default branch (merging the pull request that sets it is the human approval); `draft` is never delivered and `deprecated` is retired. Entries change by pull request only. Optional front matter: `covers` (a GitHub issue URL, on a stand-in for an upstream fix) and `stale_after` (a `YYYY-MM-DD` date).
- **delivery** - at spawn, reading entries from the guidance repo's default branch commit (never its working tree), fetched at most once per `guidance-fetch-minutes`, selecting the stable guidance entries that match a step's project and stage, within `guidance-budget-bytes`, appending them to the worker's system prompt after the step body, and recording the ids delivered and skipped on the step.
- **citation** - a worker's record, made once before it finishes with `lc attach <step-id> guidance-cited "<id>[, <id>...]"`, of the delivered guidance entries that changed what it did; engine-validated against the step's `guidance-delivered`, so an entry's deliveries can be compared with its citations.
- **guidance report** - `lc guidance [--json] [--since YYYY-MM-DD]`, a read-only verb (a worker may run it, so the material audit can read it): one row per guidance entry with its deliveries, citations, opportunity, last delivered (the `created_at` of the latest step that was delivered it) and flags, flagged rows first, plus a work summary (spawns per project with the project's facets as registered now and the steps that ran, with counts). Counts come from steps still in the store; `--since` limits deliveries, citations, opportunity and the work summary to steps created on or after the date, and the output states the window. `last_delivered` and the flags always use all-time usage. It never edits, deprecates or deletes an entry; retiring one is a pull request to the guidance repo that the human merges. A `deprecated` entry is listed with no flags.
- **opportunity** - per guidance entry, the number of spawns in the report's window whose project facets and step match the entry's `applies-to`, by delivery's own matching rule (`matches`), regardless of the entry's status. It is the upper bound on deliveries, so an entry with few deliveries and low opportunity is rarely applicable, not unused.
- **spawn** - a step carrying a `guidance-delivered` record, written for every worker spawn whose guidance selection ran, even when no entry matched. Steps that never spawn a worker carry none and are not counted.
- **unused** - a flag: the entry was delivered at least `guidance-unused-after-deliveries` times and never cited. Below that count the row carries a "too few deliveries to judge" note and no flag. A flag, never an action.
- **never-delivered** - a flag on a `stable` entry not delivered within the last `guidance-dormant-days` days, or never delivered and added to the guidance repo more than that many days ago (exactly that age does not flag; a newer entry carries a "new" note instead; an unreadable add date keeps the flag and says so). A flag, never an action.
- **covers-closed** - a flag: the entry's `covers` issue is `CLOSED` upstream. Removal waits until the fix is installed locally, which the human judges. When GitHub cannot be read the state is `unknown`, reported in the output, and the report still succeeds. A flag, never an action.
- **stale** - a flag: the entry's `stale_after` date is strictly before today's local date. An unparseable value adds a note and no flag. A flag, never an action.
- **matching rule** - an entry matches when every facet in its `applies-to.facets` is declared by the item's project (no facets matches every project; an item with no resolvable project matches only entries with no facets) and when its `applies-to.steps` is empty or names the step, either bare (`write-code`, any origin) or as `<origin>/<step>` (that origin only). Matched entries naming steps come before those naming none, each group ordered by id; an entry whose body does not fit the remaining budget is skipped whole and the walk continues.
- **scan** - `lc project scan [dir]`, read-only: walks a directory tree for git repos, classifying each as `new` (identity resolved, not yet registered), `already-registered`, or `no-remote` (no parseable GitHub remote). Registers nothing itself - a human or `lc project add` acts on its output.
- **branch update** - the engine bringing a waiting PR's branch up to its base through GitHub's update-branch API (rebase, pinned to the head SHA it just read), with no agent and no worktree. The pool's PR monitor decides it for a gate whose PR reports `BEHIND` on a base that requires up-to-date branches (strict required status checks, cached per repo and base). The monitor queues the update in **pr writes**; the **github poll** checks the rule and performs the update on its next run, and the monitor applies the outcome on the first tick after. It records the update on the gate and leaves the gate `waiting`; a refused update is not retried until the head SHA changes, and a gate is updated at most three times in a row before the engine stops and notes it (the count belongs to the gate and resets when the PR reaches a new gate). A gate that is behind and will not be updated carries a `behind-not-updated` marker that `lc inbox` and the TUI show.

## Identity

- **shortcode** - a project's id prefix (see "Project registry" above); every registered project has its own, not one engine-wide constant.
- **id nesting** - ids nest by parent: `LC-3` (item), `LC-3.1` (step) - `LC` here is one project's shortcode.

## Naming discipline

- A term used anywhere must match this doc. A new concept gets added here first; a synonym for an existing one is drift.
- **Open rename candidate:** `trace` -> `inspect` (reads more naturally as "look closely at an item end-to-end"). Decide, then apply across the CLI, docs, and step markdown in one change.
