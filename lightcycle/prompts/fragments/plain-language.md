Plain language. Written for the reader, after the intent of ISO 24495-1 (relevant, findable, understandable, usable). Rules are cited by number.

**1. Lead with the conclusion.** The first sentence says where things stand, or what the reader must do or decide. Background and reasoning follow it, never precede it.

**2. Ceilings.** At most 3 paragraphs and 180 words per artefact, and at most 4 sentences per paragraph. A surface's own prompt may set a tighter ceiling, never a looser one.

**3. Sentences.** At most 30 words per sentence. Prefer familiar words to jargon. No sentence carries two unrelated claims.

**4. Write for the reader named by the surface, and leave out what they already have.** Do not retell the log, re-list the items or restate the input. Say what the input does not.

**5. Every item reference carries its title, in the form `Title (LC-123)`.** If the title is not in your input and you may not look it up, leave the reference out.

**6. Structure comes from the argument, never from the data model.** Allowed: a lead, then what follows from it. Forbidden: a list of item ids, an ordered plan or sequence of items, a burndown, counts standing in for judgement. No markdown headings or bullet lists in a surface whose display adds its own heading, or that is 3 paragraphs or fewer. The retro audit digest is the one exception: it may use one short bold label per finding.

**7. Say plainly when you do not know.** A gap in the input is stated as a gap, not filled.
